# #s3

## Overview
This channel is primarily used for the development of BST's S3 VTOL aircraft - a long-endurance drone with vertical takeoff/landing capability. The S3 is designed as a hybrid fixed-wing/quadcopter aircraft with tilting rotors, targeting 2-3 hour endurance with automatic flight operations.

## Key Decisions

**Battery Configuration (2020-2026)**
- Selected 6S10P 18650 cell configuration using LG HG2/VTC6 cells over LiPo packs
- Established 120-second VTOL time budget (60s takeoff + 60s landing)
- Later upgraded to 12S configuration for improved performance
- Current configuration: 2x 540Wh packs (1080Wh total) for S3
- **Battery swap consideration (April 28, 2026)**: Joshua Fromm proposing battery change with constraints: swap without removing payload, no less capacity than current, no more weight than current, same mounting location (front)
  - Approved by Maciej and Jack Elston
- **Future battery evolution noted (April 30, 2026)**: Next version of battery likely to use different cells, increasing pack cost; old packs will be entirely incompatible with future aircraft
- **Battery shipping approach (May 8, 2026)**: Batteries will ship in approved cardboard box rather than ATA300 case (case identification and procurement ongoing with manufacturing partner MM). Class 9 hazmat shipment configuration. ATA300 case target: before Canada deliveries but likely after UMES deliveries. Future S3 battery versions will have custom foam inserts. Final airframe delivery may include battery case for final customer delivery.
- **High-performance battery upgrade (May 19, 2026)**: New cell configuration tested showing significant voltage improvements
  - New pack provides 5.6V additional voltage after 1-hour flight on landing, 5.4V on takeoff
  - Estimated 16% more RPM or 35% more thrust on landing vs. standard 35V nominal
  - Capacity change: 5000 mAh (old cells) vs. 6500 mAh (new cells) - represents effective capacity increase despite lower nominal rating due to ability to run lower voltages and handling of cruise loads
  - Joshua Fromm to prepare new pack cells and conduct additional testing to confirm flight time with 3V/cell landing voltage target
  - Approval given to fly methane payload with high-power pack

**Motor Selection (2023-2026)**
- Chose Vertiq 81-08 G2 motors with integrated ESCs ($700 each) for rear rotors
- Selected KDE motors as backup due to Vertiq supply delays
- Rejected T-motor options due to NDAA/China restrictions
- Up to 6 Vertiq motors available from vendor currently; company has 3 unused + 2 in use on mass model (as of April 22, 2026)
- **Deferred motor platform decision** (April 22, 2026): Decided to continue with Vertiq for now; ground short issue resolved with simple insulating kapton film spacer; revisit motor choice at group meeting
- **Approved procurement (April 28, 2026)**: Jack Elston approved Joshua Fromm purchasing 4 additional Vertiq motors (~$4,000) with invoice ready

**Design Configuration**
- Wing extension: 16" added to handle VTOL weight
- Tilting rotor design: 10-15 degree tilt angle for efficiency
- Battery placement: Moved from rear to front fuselage for CG balance
- Color scheme: Purple and gold selected for first prototype
- Tail configuration: 40 degrees from horizontal (confirmed May 8, 2026)
- **Future landing gear improvement (April 28, 2026)**: Jack Elston requested design of additional fins under motor mounts (future work, not required for next batch). Goals: remove need for wooden stand in field, enable landing in rugged terrain, improve landing safety vs. payload, aesthetic improvements
- **D-sub tail boom connector orientation (May 19, 2026)**: Joshua Fromm coordinating connector orientation for tail boom to fuselage interface; photo reference requested from team

**Control Architecture (April 22, 2026)**
- **MAJOR DECISION**: Switching from DroneCAN to PWM control for first S3 aircraft
- Rationale: Too many delays waiting to validate DroneCAN with issues on S1-22; moving to PWM allows faster progress
- Ailerons already swapped to PWM (even for last flight); remaining items are DroneCAN-related
- Joshua Fromm estimate: Less than 2 hours to complete PWM swap
- DroneCAN will remain primary for future/parallel testing on S1-22

**Servo Selection (April 2026)**
- Replaced servos with new units supporting up to 333Hz update frequency
- Left aileron on address 1, right aileron on address 4
- New servos have slightly different down deflection due to spline offset - acceptable given fixed-length linkages
- Caution: Manual upward aileron movement can overextend wipers, reducing down deflection if not caught

**Turn Coordination Control (May 11, 2026)**
- **MAJOR IMPROVEMENT**: New turn coordination controller implemented showing 35% RMS improvement in "turn coordination" error (desired vs. measured turn rate)
- Key advantage: New controller uses much less rudder than mixing approach, allowing for further gain increases
- Expected outcome: Single set of gains should work across all conditions without needing comms changes
- S1-22 can use higher gains for transition and yaw damping as needed
- Code cleanup completed by Jack Elston (May 11, 2026):
  - Fixed surface mixing logic (aileron-to-rudder mixing correction)
  - Removed redundant motor testing code
  - Maciej had tested with correct code despite uncommitted git changes
  - Ready for S1-22 testing before S3 flights

**Flight Parameters (April-May 2026)**
- Climbout throttle set to 75% (starting value, reduces if overspeeding or over climbing)
- Weight for mass model testing: 15.1kg
- Estimated full weight with payloads: 16kg
- Speed scaling: 19m/s (S2) → 21m/s (S3 at 16kg) based on mass difference
- **Refined parameters (April 28, 2026)**:
  - Takeoff consumed 17Wh with 25m safe height in 10s
  - Throttles never exceeded 75% on takeoff (acceleration-limited, not pitch-control-limited)
  - Pivoters reached 70% forward for transition
  - Landing saw some saturation (100%) for roll/pitch correction but short-lived
  - Speed of 21 m/s confirmed as good compromise: uses ~10% more power than 19m/s with reasonable pitch angle
  - Estimated power consumption: 450W under refined tuning (down from higher spread before tuning); expects further improvement with throttle gain adjustments
