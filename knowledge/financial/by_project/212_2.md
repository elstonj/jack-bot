### Key Concerns

1. **Budget Overrun:** The $1,947 specialized nose cone kit was not in the original $89,844 approved budget. This represents a 2.2% cost increase. **Concern Level: LOW** — small absolute overrun, but indicates scope change or customer add-on not pre-approved.

2. **Schedule Slip:** Launcher refurbishment was due June 30, 2026 but not completed until October 27, 2026 (4-month delay). This suggests technical difficulties (solenoid leak during pressurization required investigation and repair) or resource constraints. However, all other deliverables were completed by project end date, and the delay did not prevent final delivery.

3. **Payment Status:** QuickBooks records show $91,791.00 as "outstanding balance" as of October 12, 2026. **No payment receipt is documented in the actuals summary.** This creates cash flow risk and unclear accounts receivable aging.

4. **Labor Cost Transparency:** Asana shows 4 named contributors (Josh Fromm, Jack Elston, Spencer Hoehl) plus 9 unassigned tasks, but QuickBooks invoicing does not itemize labor hours or rates. Cannot verify if labor was over/under budget or if indirect costs are embedded in component prices.

5. **Scope Creep:** Addition of specialized carbon fiber nose cone kit (Invoice #1790) post-contract award suggests either:
   - Unplanned customer requirement
   - Payload integration need discovered mid-project
   - Change order not formally documented in budget docs provided

⚠️ **Cash flow risk: PENDING** — $91,791.00 invoiced but payment status unclear; no receipt date recorded. Follow up on NASA payment.

⚠️ **Labor & cost transparency: DATA GAPS** — QuickBooks provides component-level invoicing but not labor cost tracking or indirect rate verification. Recommend requesting labor-hour detail from Asana time tracking or internal timesheets to validate cost performance.

**Recommendation:** Project successfully delivered all hardware and services. For future similar contracts, implement:
