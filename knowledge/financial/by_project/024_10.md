  - If delivery is delayed beyond May 22, BST may incur additional labor, testing, and logistics costs **beyond the $22,337 contract value** (no contingency budget visible).
  - Customer may impose penalties or withhold payment for late delivery.
  - Training dates (July 1–4, 2026) may cascade into rescheduling costs (travel rebooking, trainer availability).

#### 🟡 **2. PRIORITY RANKING CONFLICT — S0-VTOL Competes for Limited Resources**
- **Company-Wide Context (per Maciej Stachura, 2026-04-30):** S0-VTOL ranks **#2 among top 5 BST company priorities:**
  1. S3 IRAD (end-of-May delivery target) — **HIGHER PRIORITY**
  2. **S0-VTOL (TWO deliveries: ERAU and Barbados)** ← competing for same engineering resources
  3. EMASS closeout
  4. By-Lite Mustang
  5. SBIR Magnetometer
- **Resource Conflict:** S3 IRAD has higher priority and earlier deadline (end of May 2026). S0-VTOL Barbados delivery (May 22, 2026) overlaps with S3 IRAD crunch. Engineering team bandwidth is constrained.
- **Financial Implication:** Prioritization of S3 IRAD could cause further delay to Barbados S0-VTOL, increasing cost overrun risk.

#### 🟡 **3. OPERATOR TRAINING — UNASSIGNED & NO DUE DATE**
- **Status:** Training materials and operator instruction remain unassigned with no due date, despite **July 1–4, 2026 training delivery** being scheduled.
- **Scope Clarity:** Curriculum and training materials have not been finalized; preliminary materials review was scheduled for week of May 5, 2025 (Sabu Best availability window), but no evidence of completion in provided data.
- **Financial Implication:**
  - Late assignment of training lead and curriculum development could trigger schedule slip and rework costs.
  - Travel and logistics for July 1–4, 2026 training are likely included in the lump-sum contract but unbudgeted at line-item level; any rescheduling will stress the fixed $22,337 margin.

#### 🟡 **4. HARDWARE ASSET SCARCITY — Ground Station Risk**
- **Flagged Issue (Jack Elston, 2026-04-20):** Ground station asset scarcity. Elston noted: *"depending on timeline we might be able to shift some older ground stations from the S0-VTOL kits. (I hate to do that, but might be best)."*
- **Implication:** If timeline slips further, BST may be forced to substitute lower-spec ground station hardware to meet other company priorities. This could degrade customer value or require rework/additional cost if substitution is unacceptable to Barbados Meteorological Services.
- **Financial Implication:** Potential customer dissatisfaction, warranty claims, or additional engineering effort to adapt or substitute hardware.

#### 🟡 **5. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE — ISM BAND INTERFERENCE RISK**
- **Issue Flagged (Barbados Prime Minister's Office):** 902–928 MHz ISM band interference risk. S0-VTOL uses **Microhard P900 radio (FCC ID: NS913P900, IC ID: 3143A-13P900, PN: MHS185000)** operating in unregulated ISM band with frequency-hopping (200 kHz channels).
- **Requirement:** Verification required before operational deployment to confirm mitigation of interference risk with local communications infrastructure.
- **Financial Implication:** 
  - If compliance testing is required (e.g., EMC testing, frequency coordination with Barbados regulators), additional costs may be incurred beyond the fixed contract value.
  - Delayed compliance approval could push delivery past May 22, 2026.

#### 🔴 **6. NDAA COMPLIANCE NOT FINALIZED**
- **Status (Alex Lomis, 2026-04-17):** S0 VTOL, S0 AD, and E2 platforms "can be" compliant with NDAA but are not yet "fully" compliant.
- **Implication:** If Barbados customer or U.S. regulatory bodies require NDAA compliance (e.g., for export, interoperability, or congressional oversight), additional engineering and certification work may be required.
- **Financial Implication:** Unbudgeted compliance remediation could exceed the $22,337 contract value.
