# Project 024-03 — Financial Summary

## Project Overview
- **Full Project Name**: "On-demand wildfire susceptibility from fuel moisture and vegetative index maps"
- **Client/Agency**: NASA (ROSES 2024, Topic A.59 funding opportunity)
- **Contract Type**: NASA ROSES research grant (Step 2 submission)
- **Period of Performance**: April 1, 2025 – April 1, 2028 (3 years)
- **Key Personnel**: Jack Elston (BST Founder), Maciej Stachura (BST), Ryan Ferrell (Pepperwood Reserve, Co-I), Margaret Boeger (Pepperwood President & CEO)
- **Brief Description of Scope**: Multi-partner research project integrating small UAS, NISAR satellite, and UAVSAR data to generate soil moisture and live fuel moisture (LFM) maps for wildfire susceptibility assessment. Project includes 9 field validation campaigns at Pepperwood Reserve and 3 annual Weather Stream flight campaigns. All data and codes to be archived and released publicly upon validation.

### Key Observations

#### Strengths
1. **Clear Budget Allocation**: Budgets are well-detailed by cost category (labor, materials, travel, indirect) for both partner organizations (Pepperwood and Weather Stream).
2. **Defined Effort Levels**: Personnel effort is quantified in hours and annual weeks for each team member, enabling burn-rate tracking once the project starts.
3. **Reasonable Indirect Cost Rate**: 20% indirect rate is within NASA allowable limits and is standard for research organizations.
4. **Travel Costs Justified**: Weather Stream annual visits to Pepperwood Reserve are specifically tied to programmatic coordination needs (3-year flights, field campaigns).

#### Concerns / Risks
1. **No Black Swift Technologies Direct Budget Visible**: The budget docs show Pepperwood Reserve and Weather Stream budgets but do not include a separate Black Swift Technologies institutional budget allocation. Since BST is listed as the prime contractor/submitting organization and provides the UAS platform, there should be a BST-level budget component (e.g., UAS development, payload support, platform maintenance). This data may exist in unreported budget documents.
   
2. **No QuickBooks Actuals Available**: Without actual invoice, expense, and payment data, we cannot currently assess:
   - Whether spending is tracking to budget
   - Cash flow status or payment delays
   - Whether specific cost categories are overrunning
   - Actual burn rate vs. planned burn rate
   
3. **Project Status Ambiguity**: The Proposal document (dated 2024-03-07, modified 2024-03-25) is archived in an "Attic" folder with a note stating "suggests the proposal was not ultimately submitted or was abandoned after initial development." However, the budget documents reference it as an active award with a PoP of April 1, 2025 – April 1, 2028. **Clarification needed**: Is this proposal awarded or still pending?

4. **Sentry/Crowheart Project Confusion**: Drive budget docs include a separate "Sentry/Crowheart" cost model ($19,000 NRE + operations). This is explicitly documented as a different project but was mixed into the same folder. No financial linkage to 024-03 is documented, but the presence of this document could indicate budget tracking issues or folder organization issues.

5. **No CLINs or Government Line Items**: Unlike some government contracts, this NASA grant structure does not include CLINs. While typical for grants, it means budget variance tracking will rely entirely on QBO cost-code mapping rather than government-provided line item structure.

6. **Contingency Budget Not Documented**: Neither budget document explicitly shows a contingency or management reserve. For a 3-year research project with field campaigns and multiple data sources, unknown risks could impact budget.
