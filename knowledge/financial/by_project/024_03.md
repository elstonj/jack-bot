# Project 024-03 — Financial Summary

## Project Overview

**Project Title:** "On-demand wildfire susceptibility from fuel moisture and vegetative index maps"

**Client/Agency:** NASA (ROSES 2024, A.59 funding opportunity)

**Contract Type:** NASA ROSES research grant (Step 2 submission)

**Period of Performance:** April 1, 2025 – April 1, 2028 (3 years)

**Description of Scope:** Multi-institutional research project integrating small UAS platforms, satellite data (NISAR, UAVSAR), and ground validation campaigns to generate soil moisture and live fuel moisture (LFM) maps for wildfire susceptibility assessment. Project involves field validation campaigns at Pepperwood Preserve, data processing and synthesis, and open science data management.

### Risk Assessment & Concerns

**Positive Indicators:**
- ✓ **Reasonable personnel cost allocation:** Labor rates ($30–$150/hr) are within range for research and field operations.
- ✓ **Modest overhead rate:** 20% indirect cost rate is at maximum allowable and well-justified for research institution support.
- ✓ **Clear resource allocation:** Distinct roles (field scientists, technicians, project managers, flight support) with defined effort hours.
- ✓ **Detailed field operations budget:** Specific lodging, fuel, and equipment needs identified for 9 field campaigns.

**Potential Concerns:**
1. **Multi-institutional coordination:** Project spans Pepperwood, Weather Stream, Black Swift Technologies, and other partners. Coordination overhead not explicitly budgeted; risk of cost overruns if inter-organizational communication/travel costs materialize.

2. **Equipment/UAS costs not detailed:** Budget documents do not itemize the cost of small UAS platforms, sensors, avionics, or integration work. Implied Black Swift partner budget (~$79,967) is a residual; no detailed breakdown provided. **Risk:** If UAS platform costs are underestimated, project could exceed budget.

3. **Data processing scalability:** Weather Stream effort is capped at ~185 hrs/year for flight campaign and data processing support. With 192 UAS flights/year budgeted, this equates to ~1.15 hours per flight for data processing/validation. **Risk:** If data processing requirements exceed estimate (e.g., due to higher error rates, additional validation rounds, QA/QC), Weather Stream labor could overrun.

4. **Long lead times for satellite data:** Project depends on NISAR satellite operational status (October 2025 onward). Any delay in NISAR operational readiness could impact data integration timeline and downstream deliverables without budget adjustment.

5. **Concurrent commitments (from Current & Pending Support):** PI Jack Elston and key team member Maciej Stachura have significant concurrent commitments (~$1.73M active, ~$1.25M pending) with combined effort allocations of 45%+ per year. **Risk:** Project 024-03 effort allocations may be challenged if concurrent projects experience delays or scope creep.

6. **No contingency explicitly identified:** Budget documents do not show a contingency reserve or management reserve. Standard practice for research grants is 10–15% contingency; none is evident here.
