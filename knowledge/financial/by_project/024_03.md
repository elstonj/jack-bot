# Project 024-03 — Financial Summary

## Project Overview
- **Full Project Name**: "On-demand wildfire susceptibility from fuel moisture and vegetative index maps"
- **Client/Funding Agency**: NASA (ROSES 2024, A.59 funding opportunity)
- **Contract Type**: NASA ROSES research grant (Step 2 submission)
- **Period of Performance**: April 1, 2025 – April 1, 2028 (3 years)
- **Key Partners**: 
  - Pepperwood (field operations, data synthesis)
  - Weather Stream (airborne data collection, 3 flights/year)
  - Black Swift Technologies (coordinating partner, UAS platform, systems integration)
- **Brief Scope**: Development of on-demand wildfire susceptibility maps by integrating fuel moisture measurements (via small UAS, 192 flights/year at 2m resolution), live vegetative index data (NISAR satellite, UAVSAR), and field validation campaigns (9 campaigns/year) to support fire management agencies and operational decision-making.

### Key Financial Concerns & Risks

1. **Missing BST Budget Allocation** ⚠️
   - **Issue**: BST is identified as the coordinating partner and mentioned in the project overview, but no dedicated BST budget appears in the provided Drive Budget Documents.
   - **Risk**: BST labor, overhead, or systems integration costs may not be fully accounted for. This could lead to cost overruns or underfunded project activities.
   - **Recommendation**: Obtain BST budget allocation from NASA ROSES award letter or updated budget documents.

2. **High Personnel Effort Concentration**
   - **Issue**: Pepperwood relies heavily on two individuals: Ryan Ferrell (1,000 hrs/yr = 48% FTE) and Makayla Freed (850 hrs/yr = 41% FTE). Weather Stream project lead (110 hrs/yr = 5.8% effort) is lower but still material.
   - **Risk**: Key personnel departures, illness, or competing commitments could impact schedule and require rebudgeting.
   - **Mitigation**: Cross-training and documented procedures for field campaigns.

3. **Field Campaign Dependencies**
   - **Issue**: 9 field validation campaigns per year at Pepperwood require coordination between Ryan Ferrell, Makayla Freed, and external stakeholders. Weather and logistics could cause delays.
   - **Risk**: Campaign delays could compress post-processing and data synthesis timelines, affecting deliverables.
   - **Mitigation**: Build contingency into campaign schedule; plan campaigns during optimal field seasons.

4. **Equipment Serviceability Risk**
   - **Issue**: Field equipment (bench scale, pole pruners, Nalgene bottles) purchased in Year 1 may require replacement or repair in Years 2–3, but no maintenance or replacement budget is allocated.
   - **Risk**: Unbudgeted equipment costs if items fail or wear out.
   - **Mitigation**: Obtain equipment warranties; budget 10–15% equipment maintenance reserve in future budget revisions.

5. **NISAR & UAVSAR Data Dependency**
   - **Issue**: Project relies on external NASA satellite and airborne platforms (NISAR operational Oct 2025, UAVSAR 9 flights Year 1). If these platforms experience delays or failures, project milestones could slip.
   - **Risk**: External dependencies could cascade into delays in soil moisture and live fuel moisture map generation.
   - **Mitigation**: Prioritize small UAS platform (192 flights/year) as primary data source; treat NISAR/UAVSAR as complementary.

6. **No Contingency or Management Reserve**
   - **Issue**: Proposed budgets (Pepperwood and Weather Stream) appear fully allocated with no explicit contingency or management reserve line item.
   - **Risk**: Any unanticipated costs (additional travel, equipment, personnel overtime) could exceed budget without formal contingency.
   - **Recommendation**: Retain 5–10% management reserve from approved budget or request budget amendment if needed.
