- **Full Project Name**: "On-demand wildfire susceptibility from fuel moisture and vegetative index maps"

### Key Budget Observations

**1. Personnel Dominance (46.9% direct labor + 19.3% fringe = 66.2% of total budget)**
   - Pepperwood partner is 85.7% personnel-driven (personnel + fringe = $204.3K of $198.4K is actually $204.3K including fringe as shown in subtotal)
   - **Interpretation**: This is a labor-intensive research project. Cost overruns will most likely stem from personnel hour overages or rate increases.
   - **Concern**: Ensure that personnel availability (particularly Ryan Ferrell at 48% FTE and Makayla Freed at 41% FTE) is maintained for the full 3-year period. Competing commitments documented in "Current and Pending Support" section show Jack Elston and Maciej Stachura have 45% combined effort on active projects + 15% on a pending $1.25M DoD-AFWERX project.

**2. Indirect Costs (14.1% of total, $73,054.40)**
   - Both partners use 20% F&A (maximum allowed under NASA ROSES).
   - **Concern**: If actual indirect cost rate is lower than 20%, project has built-in underutilization. If higher, this could trigger budget issues.

**3. Field Operations (Pepperwood)**
   - Lodging + Fuel + Field Supplies = $6,455 (3.3% of Pepperwood budget)
   - **Observation**: This is modest relative to labor costs, suggesting field campaigns are relatively short duration and geographically concentrated at Pepperwood Preserve.

**4. Travel (Weather Stream)**
   - Only $2,974/year ($8,922 total 3-year) for annual programmatic visit.
   - **Observation**: Minimal travel budget; rely on virtual coordination. Risk: Any in-person meeting beyond one annual visit would exceed budget.

**5. Equipment & Supplies (Pepperwood)**
   - Total $1,955 over 3 years (~$652/year).
   - **Observation**: Very lean. No consumables budget for field supplies beyond what's itemized. Risk: Unexpected field equipment failures or sample kit replacements could exceed allocation.
