# Project 024-03 — Financial Summary

## Project Overview

**Full Project Name:**  
"On-demand wildfire susceptibility from fuel moisture and vegetative index maps"

**Client/Agency:**  
NASA (ROSES 2024, Opportunity A.59)

**Contract Type:**  
NASA ROSES research grant (Step 2 submission)

**Period of Performance:**  
April 1, 2025 – April 1, 2028 (3 years)

**Brief Description of Scope:**  
Multi-institutional research project combining field validation campaigns and remote sensing (small UAS, NISAR satellite, UAVSAR) to develop on-demand wildfire susceptibility products derived from fuel moisture and vegetative index maps. Partners include Pepperwood Preserve, Weather Stream, and Black Swift Technologies (BST). The project includes 9 field campaigns per year at Pepperwood, 192 UAS flights per year, and data processing in IDL and Python to produce soil moisture and live fuel moisture products for public release.

### Key Budget Risks & Observations

1. **Budget Scaling Question:**  
   The Pepperwood budget document shows Year 1 annual costs of $198,426; however, the Weather Stream document specifies a 3-year extension with Year 1 cost of $79,966.80 multiplied by 3 years = $239,900.40 total. This suggests Weather Stream costs are fixed across the 3-year period, but Pepperwood's budget does not clearly specify whether its $198,426 is annual or total. **Clarification needed:** Does Pepperwood's $198,426 apply to each year, or is it the total for 3 years?

2. **Labor Effort Sustainability:**  
   Pepperwood's personnel allocations include significant effort from key staff:
   - Ryan Ferrell (Research Scientist): 1,000 hours/year ($55,000/year) = 8.25 weeks/year
   - Makayla Freed (Field Technician): 850 hours/year ($25,500/year) = 7 weeks/year
   
   These are substantial annual commitments. If repeated across 3 years without additional budget growth, salary escalation could create shortfall. Budget documents do not account for annual cost-of-living adjustments or merit increases.

3. **Field Campaign Operations:**  
   9 field campaigns per year with 70 nights of lodging at $3,500/year and vehicle fuel at $1,000/year appear reasonable for a preserve-based research program. However, no contingency is specified for weather delays, equipment failures, or scope expansion.

4. **Indirect Cost Rate:**  
   Both Pepperwood and Weather Stream use a 20% indirect cost rate (maximum allowed for many federal programs). This is appropriate and compliant but should be monitored for actual cost recovery.

5. **Data Delivery & External Dependencies:**  
   Success depends on:
   - NASA NISAR satellite operational readiness (expected October 2025, post-project start)
   - UAVSAR availability and scheduling (NASA-coordinated, no cost to this project but schedule risk)
   - NOAA/NESDIS data access for live fuel moisture validation
   
   Delays in these external data sources could impact deliverable timelines and may require budget reallocation.

6. **Partner Cost Distributions:**  
   - Pepperwood: 38.3% of total budget (field/ground truth work)
   - Weather Stream: 46.3% of total budget (3-year allocation; ~15.4% per year)
   - BST (inferred): ~15.4% (assumed based on aggregate less identified partners)
   
   No itemized BST budget is provided in source documents, making it difficult to assess BST's cost structure.
