# Project 024-03 — Financial Summary

## Project Overview
- **Full Project Name**: "On-demand wildfire susceptibility from fuel moisture and vegetative index maps"
- **Client/Agency**: NASA (ROSES 2024, A.59 funding opportunity)
- **Contract Type**: NASA ROSES research grant (Step 2 submission)
- **Period of Performance**: April 1, 2025 – April 1, 2028 (3 years)
- **Project Description**: Research project integrating small UAS, NISAR satellite, and UAVSAR data to generate high-resolution soil moisture and live fuel moisture maps for wildfire risk assessment. Two primary partners: Pepperwood (field validation, data synthesis) and Weather Stream (flight operations, data processing).

**Cannot assess without QuickBooks data.** The following analysis is based on proposed budget structure only:

- **Annual burn rate (Y1)**: ~$146,109 or ~$12,176/month

#### Budget Risks & Observations

**Positive Indicators (from proposed budget)**:
1. **Personnel effort is realistic & distributed**: Field campaigns (9/year) with dedicated technician + research scientist; operations support proportional to flight frequency
2. **Equipment purchases are one-time**: Bench scale ($1,000) and tools appear to be capital items absorbed in Year 1
3. **Low overhead**: 20% IDC is efficient for university/nonprofit research
4. **Modest travel**: Single annual visit by Weather Stream team limits cost surprise

**Potential Concerns (awaiting actual spending data)**:
1. **No cost overrun buffer identified**: Budget is tightly constrained; any unbudgeted expenses (equipment failure, additional field campaigns, personnel changes) could exceed allocation
2. **Personnel cost sensitivity**: $158,900 Pepperwood personnel budget is 80% of Pepperwood allocation; any scope creep in field campaigns could drive labor overruns
3. **Subcontractor costs not visible**: No mention of subcontractors for NISAR/UAVSAR data processing, sensor integration, or external labs—costs may be absorbed in indirect or embedded in partner budgets
4. **Equipment/software licensing**: Proposal mentions IDL and Python codes; software licenses (IDL: expensive) not explicitly budgeted
5. **Partnership dependencies**: Weather Stream flight costs ($192 flights/year) depend on aircraft availability and fuel prices; no contingency visible
