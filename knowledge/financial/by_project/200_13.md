### Key Concerns:

1. **Labor Overrun Unsustainable:** Field deployment in May required 44–60% more labor hours than estimated for two key personnel. If similar demands occur in June–August (final demonstrations, post-field analysis, report preparation), labor costs will exceed budget by $10,000–$15,000+.

2. **Fixed Overhead on Variable Labor:** Indirect costs (overhead at 46.61%, G&A at 18.32%) are applied to the inflated labor base, further compounding the overage. Every dollar of labor overrun carries an additional ~65% in indirect costs.

3. **Insufficient Budget Buffer:** Contract value ($80,972) leaves no contingency for field deployment challenges. Real-world issues (equipment repair, extended troubleshooting, training) are already pushing spend above ceiling in May.

4. **Payment Timing Risk:** Large invoices issued ($39,310 and $39,309) but payment status unknown. If NASA delays payment, BST may need to fund operations with internal cash while awaiting reimbursement.

5. **Reporting vs. Spending Timeline Misalignment:** Final invoice (#1669) issued July 21 for $39,309, but period of performance ends August 20. Any costs incurred in late July–August may not be fully captured in invoicing or may require modification/supplemental invoice.

### Recommendations:

- **Immediate:** Confirm payment status of invoices #1659, #1702, and #1669 from NASA
- **Short-term:** Implement strict labor hour tracking and reforecasting for June–August to identify if additional overage is likely
- **Contractual:** If labor overrun continues, consider whether formal modification or supplemental agreement with NASA is warranted (though may be difficult given fixed-price nature of contract)
- **Future:** Include 10–15% contingency in labor budgets for field deployment projects requiring real-time problem-solving and training
