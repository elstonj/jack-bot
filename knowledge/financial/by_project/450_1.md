### **Labor**
- **Components**: 
  - Project Management (Jack Elston, CEO): 2 hrs per deployment @ $174/hr
  - Flight Crew Operations (Pilot, Observer/Technician): 7 days per deployment @ $1,500/day

- **Proposed Total**: $88,116.00 ($50,352 Y1 + $37,764 Y2)
- **Actual Total**: $90,288.00 ($51,860 Y1 + $38,428 Y2)
- **Status**: **OVER BUDGET by $2,172.00 (102.5% of budget)**
  - Consistent 2–3% overage in both years
  - Likely due to extended crew deployment days or additional processing hours

### **Travel**
- **Components**: 
  - Overhead (logistics, planning): $125 per deployment instance × 2 instances per deployment
  - Meals: 14 days per deployment @ $76/day per diem (2 people) = $2,128/deployment
  - Lodging: 12 nights per deployment @ $175/night (2 people, 6 nights each) = $2,100/deployment

- **Actual Total**: $19,423.63 ($11,468.64 Y1 + $7,954.99 Y2)

  - Year One used 78% of travel budget
  - Year Two used 72% of travel budget
  - Suggests fewer crew members deployed, shorter stays, or negotiated per diem rates than planned

## Financial Health Assessment

### **Overall Status: EXCELLENT – Project Closed On Budget**

✅ **Strengths:**
1. **Exceptional budget control**: Project invoiced at 99.99% of contract value ($17.37 variance on $148.7K contract)
2. **Year One completion under budget**: Delivered $659.36 under budgeted amount despite operational complexity
3. **Clean project closure**: Final invoice submitted 11/29/2023, matching end of Period of Performance (11/29/2023)
4. **Disciplined spending patterns**: Actual invoices track closely to planned deployment schedule and budget structure

### **Areas of Attention:**

⚠️ **Cost Overruns:**
- **Equipment Rental**: Year Two exceeded budget by $3,000 (120% of budget). This suggests:
  - Additional flight days were required beyond the planned 5 days/deployment
  - Possible aircraft rate increases or rental of backup/specialized equipment
  - **Recommendation**: Review deployment logs to understand if scope expanded or weather delays extended rental periods
  
- **Labor**: Both years exceeded budget by 2–3% ($1,508 in Y1, $664 in Y2):
  - Likely driven by extended flight crew operations or additional data processing hours
  - Within typical project variance but worth monitoring for pattern in future proposals

✅ **Cost Savings:**
- **Travel**: Substantial savings in both years (Y1: 78%, Y2: 72% of budget):
  - Year One saved $3,167.36; Year Two saved $3,022.01
  - Total travel savings: **$6,189.37**
  - May reflect shorter crew stays, negotiated lodging rates, or reduced crew size on some deployments
  - These savings offset the equipment and labor overruns, keeping project on overall budget

### **Key Observations:**

1. **Year Two Tighter Margin**: Year Two finished 0.9% over budget ($641.99), while Year One was under. This suggests:
   - Year Two scope may have been more operationally challenging (3 vs. 4 deployments, potentially in different terrain/weather)
   - Year Two equipment costs significantly higher ($18K vs. $21K in Y1, despite 3 vs. 4 deployments) — possible rate increases or extended operations

2. **Invoice Submission & Payment Status**:
   - **Data not available from QuickBooks** on actual payment receipt or cash-flow timing
   - Budget Docs show submission dates but not payment dates
   - Invoice 1535, 1566, 1580, 1586 have no detailed documentation — amounts inferred from Budget Tracking spreadsheet only
   - **Recommendation**: Cross-check with QuickBooks or University of Colorado payment records to confirm all invoices were paid in full

3. **Project Scope Alignment**:
   - Proposed budget in Subrecipient Commitment Form ($148,729) matches contracted amount exactly, indicating well-scoped negotiations
   - Original 6-week proposal ($164,093.50 with 15% overhead) was reduced to year-based deployment model, suggesting scope reduction during contract negotiation
