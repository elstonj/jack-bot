# Project 400-6 — Financial Summary

## Project Overview
- **Full Project Name:** "Field-Deployable Manufacturing of UAS Composite Parts" / "Adaptive UAS Manufacturing Initiative"
- **Client/Agency:** AFWERX (Air Force)
- **Prime Contractor:** Curable Composites
- **BST Role:** Subcontractor/Technical Partner
- **Contract Type:** SBIR Phase I (AFWERX X24.7)
- **Period of Performance:** February 3, 2025 – May 2, 2025 (3 months)
- **Brief Description:** Black Swift Technologies (BST) is providing technical expertise under Curable Composites' SBIR Phase I award to develop field-deployable manufacturing processes for UAS composite parts. Phase I demonstrates proof-of-concept for adaptive composite manufacturing with a focus on hollow composite cylinders for fuselage applications (TRL 3–4).

## Financial Health Assessment

### Overall Budget Status: **SIGNIFICANTLY UNDER BUDGET**

**Summary:**
Project 400-6 is in excellent financial health. As of October 2025, the project is tracking well below the $24,556.00 approved subcontract value, with only $969.59 in direct labor actually charged through August 2025.

### Key Findings:

#### 1. **Labor Performance vs. Budget**
- **Budgeted Labor:** $14,156.00 (73 hours @ $150/hr with 29.28% fringe)
- **Actual Labor Charged (Jan–Aug 2025):** $969.59 (5 hours)
- **Variance:** $(13,186.41) (6.8% of budget used)
- **Status:** ✓ **Significantly under budget**

**Analysis:**
- Only 5 out of 73 budgeted hours have been logged through August 2025 (6.8% utilization).
- Minimal activity in February–March despite the stated PoP start date of 02/03/2025.
- 4 hours charged in April coincide with final report preparation/submission (04/16/2025).
- No hours recorded May–August despite PoP extending through 05/02/2025.
- This pattern suggests either **backend-loaded work** (concentrated effort in final weeks) or **reporting lag** (hours not yet recorded in tracking system).

**Risk:** If the full 73 budgeted hours were to be charged retroactively, they would total ~$14,156.00 in direct labor cost alone, which is well within budget. However, the lack of recorded hours raises questions about:
- Whether work was actually performed as planned.
- Whether time tracking is complete and current.
- Whether there are schedule or scope delays.

#### 2. **Total Project Spend vs. Budget**
- **Approved Subcontract Value:** $24,556.00
- **Invoiced to Date:** $24,556.00 (Invoice 1664, 04/14/2025)
- **Payments Received:** $12,500.00 (interim payment, September 2025)
- **Outstanding Balance:** $12,056.00
- **Actual Expenses Recorded (Labor Only):** $969.59
- **Status:** ✓ **Under budget overall; invoice issued at full contract value**

**Analysis:**
- The full contract amount of $24,556.00 has been invoiced to Curable Composites upon submission of the CLIN0002 Final Report (04/16/2025), reflecting a **fixed-price, milestone-based billing structure**.
- An interim payment of $12,500.00 was received in September 2025 (4.5 months after invoice date), suggesting Curable Composites is managing cash flow with partial/milestone-based payments.
- Only $969.59 in direct labor has been recorded as actual expense through August 2025, creating a **$23,586.41 gap** between invoiced amount and tracked labor costs.

**Key Observation:**
The invoice amount of $24,556.00 includes not only direct labor ($14,156.00) but also proposed overhead (46.61% = $6,598.00) and G&A (18.32% = $3,802.00). These indirect costs are built into the invoice and payment structure but are not separately tracked as actual expenses in the monthly burn-rate data. This is **normal for cost-plus government contracts** where indirect rates are applied to direct costs and billed as part of the total invoice.
