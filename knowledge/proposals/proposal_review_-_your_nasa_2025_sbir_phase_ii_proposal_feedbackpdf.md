# NASA 2025 SBIR Phase II Proposal Feedback

## Document Metadata
- Type: Proposal review/feedback letter
- Client/Agency: NASA
- Program/Solicitation: NASA Program Year 2025 SBIR Phase II (Topic A2.02-1017 - Adaptive and Secure Autonomy for UAS: A Modular Approach to Flight Control, Machine Learning Integration, and Multi-Vehicle Coordination)
- Date: September 19, 2026
- BST Products/Systems Referenced: RTA (Runtime Assurance) system, Safe Sandbox supervisor
- Key Personnel: Beck Cotter (recipient)

## Executive Summary
NASA provided unfunded feedback on BST's Phase II SBIR proposal focused on Runtime Assurance (RTA) and autonomous flight control systems for UAS. The reviewers rated the proposal as "very good," praising the technical approach and team qualifications, but identified concerns about the scope of applying RTA across multiple aircraft platforms and lack of clarity on funding divisions with other contracts.

## Technical Approach
BST proposed to extend Phase I research into a ground-tested RTA implementation with the following key elements:

- **Runtime Assurance (RTA) System**: Innovative approach to safely implementing autonomous control
- **Safe Sandbox Supervisor**: Novel method for safely training AI algorithms without risk to flight operations
- **Hierarchical Modular Architecture**: Enables applicability across multiple airframes and facilitates integration with various vehicle platforms
- **Customizable Integration**: System design allows customers to incorporate their own code

**Phase I Demonstrated Results**: The RTA supervisor successfully arrested two incidents in autonomy-based control systems, demonstrating comprehensive technical competence.

## Products & Capabilities Described

### Runtime Assurance (RTA) System
- **What it is**: A safety supervisor system for autonomous control
- **Proposed use**: Ground-tested implementation for Phase II development; extension of Phase I research
- **Key capability**: Ability to safely train AI algorithms and arrest control system failures; demonstrated effectiveness in catching two autonomy-related incidents

### Safe Sandbox Supervisor
- **What it is**: A method for safely training AI algorithms
- **Proposed use**: Integration with RTA for safe AI development and validation
- **Advantage**: Allows safe testing without risk to flight operations

## Use Cases & Applications
- Small UAS for autonomy applications
- Atmospheric sciences missions
- Integration of UAS into the National Airspace System (NAS)
- AI-related control applications where demonstration and safety are critical
- Multi-vehicle coordination

## Reviewer Assessment by Factor

### Factor 1: Scientific/Technical Merit and Feasibility
**Strengths:**
- Innovative and robust RTA approach extending recent research
- Strong understanding of RTA strengths, weaknesses, state of the art, and associated risks
- Hierarchical modular architecture enables multi-airframe applicability
- Compelling Phase I results with demonstrated supervisor effectiveness
- Customer code integration capability enhances commercial appeal

**Weaknesses:**
- Limited exploration of alternative approaches (safe reinforcement learning, fault-tolerant control)
- Risk associated with applying RTA across multiple aircraft platforms during NASA AFSRB/CAS approval process
- Single-platform demonstration may have been more prudent for initial viability establishment

### Factor 2: Experience, Qualifications, and Facilities
**Strengths:**
- Strong team combination of experience and technical expertise
- History of successful SBIR projects and ongoing technology stack development
- Well-qualified personnel with sufficient commitment and availability
- Necessary instrumentation and facilities in-house; no NASA resources required
- Well-organized proposal with clear task breakdown

**Weaknesses:** None identified

### Factor 3: Effectiveness of the Proposed Work Plan
**Strengths:**
- Well-structured 24-month plan for team of 5+ members
- Tasks within scope with manageable technical risk
- Reasonable cost
- Clear objectives and acceptance criteria enabling objective NASA assessment
- Effective resource utilization
- Well-positioned for future integration into NASA autonomy, atmospheric sciences, and NAS integration projects
- Highly relevant for AI control applications emphasizing both demonstration and safety

**Weaknesses:**
- Unclear delineation between activities and other contracts (including DOE-funded work)
- Ambiguous division of funding responsibilities and potential de-prioritization of NASA interests
- Small company size and limited personnel raise concerns about capacity to deliver given other contract commitments
- Some NASA projects cited in proposal are no longer active, complicating future integration due to shifting mission directorate priorities

### Factor 4: Commercialization and Business Planning
**Strengths:**
- Very good overall proposal inspiring confidence in company viability and ability
- Particularly strong discussion of market, customers, and competitive landscape
- Clear and compelling commercialization narrative
- Layered approach with multiple potential revenue streams
- Discussion of existing partners and pending awards that would augment Phase II and accelerate commercialization

**Weaknesses:**
- Lack of detailed commercialization timeline
- Insufficient information on future capitalization needs and amounts
- Inadequate timing and revenue projections
- Limited discussion of IP protection rigor
- Unclear picture of firm's financial or capitalization status

## Notable Details
- The proposal was **not funded** despite positive reviews; limited NASA funding availability noted
- Reviewer comments emphasize BST's vertical integration and extensive flight history as potential mitigators of multi-aircraft platform risk
- Proposal positioning is appropriate for future NASA projects involving small UAS autonomy
- BST has multiple ongoing contracts and partnerships (DOE-funded work referenced) that create complexity around funding and prioritization
- NASA encouraged BST to use feedback to strengthen future submissions
