## Executive Summary
BST presented progress on Phase I Option work for a Navy SBIR program developing an expendable sonobuoy-launched UAS for anti-submarine warfare (ASW) operations. The presentation focused on two main efforts: integrating and evaluating magnetometer sensors (Bartington UAS-MAG and QuSpin) for magnetic anomaly detection, and modifying the S0-AD UAS airframe for repeated ground testing and developing a ground-launch capability.

## Technical Approach

### Magnetometer Integration
- Implemented new firmware to log DRONECAN outputs from Bartington UAS-MAG sensor
- Sensor outputs X, Y, Z magnetic vector measurements at 200 Hz
- Adapted DRONECAN to USB conversion for CSV data logging
- Characterized Bartington hardware as "significantly smaller" than QuSpin alternative
- Created integrated ground testing rig with carbon fiber boom to vary magnetometer standoff distance from airframe
- Integrated Raspberry Pi logging board for autonomous magnetic signature data recording

### Airframe Modifications for Reusability
- Reinforced folding wings to withstand repeated landings
- Replaced long-range antenna with short-range adhesive flex antenna to prevent landing damage
- Reduced aircraft weight by substituting long-endurance battery with smaller pack (~30 min flight duration)
- Developed portable elastic-driven launch rail to replicate air-drop launch profile without requiring air deployment

## Products & Capabilities Described

### S0-AD UAS
- Expendable unmanned aerial vehicle typically air-deployed or dropped from multirotor
- Originally single-use system; BST created reusable testing variant
- Modified to survive repeated ground launches and landings while maintaining form factor
- Capable of ground launch via elastic-driven rail at specified acceleration/velocity profiles

### Bartington UAS-MAG Magnetometer
- Compact sensor with DRONECAN interface (200 Hz output rate)
- Issues noted: 10 nT resolution steps (resolution problems being addressed)
- Smaller form factor compared to QuSpin alternative

### QuSpin Magnetometer
- Alternative magnetometer evaluated alongside Bartington
- Measured noise floor performance:
  - **With 20 Hz LPF:** 1-10 Hz: 5.4 pT/√Hz; 10-50 Hz: 3.5 pT/√Hz; 50-100 Hz: 3.0 pT/√Hz
  - **No filter:** 1-10 Hz: 60.1 pT/√Hz; 10-50 Hz: 47.9 pT/√Hz; 50-100 Hz: 34.0 pT/√Hz

## Use Cases & Applications
- **ASW Cued Search, Detection, Tracking, and Classification:** Primary application for sonobuoy-deployed UAS
- **Magnetic anomaly detection:** Magnetometer serves as primary sensor payload for submarine/vessel detection
- **Operational deployment:** Typically launched from sonobuoys or heavy-lift multirotor platforms; ground launch capability enables testing/demonstrations

## Key Results

### Magnetometer Testing Progress
- Successfully implemented logging of Bartington sensor outputs
- Began long-term baseline testing and noise spectral density analysis
- Identified aliasing issues at higher frequencies in initial testing
- Planned to conduct follow-up tests with 100 Hz filters to address noise floor
- QuSpin sensor demonstrated acceptable noise floor performance across frequency bands with filtering

### Airframe Modifications Progress
- Completed reinforcement design for reusable S0-AD test airframe
- Completed antenna and battery modifications for gentler landing profile
- Ground launcher concept analyzed; force/velocity/distance/time parameters calculated
- Elastic-driven launch rail designed to replicate SLC (likely sonobuoy launch container) launch profile

## Notable Details
- **Reusability trade-off:** Testing variant sacrifices performance for reusability; distinct from operational expendable variant
- **Sensor comparison:** Bartington chosen for smaller size but experiencing resolution degradation; QuSpin shows superior noise characteristics but larger form factor
- **Ground testing infrastructure:** Custom aircraft rig with variable boom-mounted sensor capability allows systematic magnetic signature characterization
- **Launch method:** Transition from air-deployment to ground-launch enables more efficient testing cadence and repeated demonstrations
- **Next priorities:** Troubleshoot Bartington logging, complete ground launcher, integrate magnetometer on airframe, conduct repeated local flights before upcoming demonstrations
