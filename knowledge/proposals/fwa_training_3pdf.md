## Notable Details

**Fraud Definition & Context:**
- Fraud is false representation about material facts or intentional deception to unlawfully deprive the U.S. government of value or to secure unearned benefits.
- Three points where fraud can occur in SBIR/STTR: during application, within the proposal itself, and during the award period.

**Four Main Types of Fraud:**
1. Misrepresentations/factual omissions to obtain funding (false certifications, inaccurate labor rates, falsified references, ineligible PIs)
2. Misrepresentation of expended funds (falsifying work done, results, or compliance; research recycling)
3. Misuse of award funds (personal use, funneling to personal accounts, falsifying timesheets)
4. Duplication of funds (multiple awards from same/different agencies for same research)

**Additional fraud types:** Self-dealing (providing subcontracts to entities with PI/family financial interest), failure to comply with federal cost principles.

**Legal Consequences:**
- Criminal: False Statements (up to 5 years prison, $250K fine); Theft of Federal Property (up to 10 years, $250K); Wire Fraud (up to 20 years, $250K); False Claims (up to $250K individual/$500K company)
- Civil: Treble damages (3x actual damages) plus $11,000 per false claim
- Administrative: Contract termination, debarment for 3+ years from federal contracts

**Prevention Measures:**
- For small businesses: Maintain detailed records (timesheets, receipts, invoices, lab notebooks, facility agreements, personnel logs); provide regular project status updates; obtain required certifications about company eligibility and non-duplication of federally funded work
- For government: Maintain open communication, follow established policies, review work performed and contract terms, conduct site visits and records audits
