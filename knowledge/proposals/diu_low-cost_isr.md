# DIU Low-Cost ISR Prize Challenge

## Document Metadata
- **Type:** Prize Challenge Solicitation / RFP
- **Client/Agency:** Department of War (DoW), Defense Innovation Unit (DIU)
- **Program/Solicitation:** Low-Cost ISR (LC-ISR) Prize Challenge
- **Date:** September 29, 2026 (release date); October 14, 2026 (submission deadline)
- **BST Products/Systems Referenced:** None mentioned in this document
- **Key Personnel:** Travis DeMeester (DIU contractor, mentioned in guidance video)

## Executive Summary
The Department of War's Innovation Unit is conducting a prize challenge to identify inexpensive, capable Unmanned Aerial Systems (UAS) and other aerial platforms capable of collecting and transmitting multi-intelligence sensor data for real-time operational and targeting decisions across land, littoral, and maritime environments. The competition offers $100 million in total prize and follow-on funding, with an additional $250 million budgeted for development, production scaling, and theater integration. Two sequential fly-offs (Q2 FY27 and Q3 FY27) will evaluate medium-endurance (3-12+ hours) and long-endurance (12-24+ hours) platforms against demonstrated capability and cost-effectiveness criteria.

## Challenge Overview & Problem Statement

**Background Problem:**
- Current ISR solutions are tethered to vulnerable airfields, require large manning, and are too expensive to proliferate
- Exquisite nature and low manufacturing throughput of legacy systems hinder employment in contested areas
- DoW struggles to meet aggregate ISR demands across joint force

**Government Objective:**
Maximize aggregate mission coverage and operational effect at the lowest total lifecycle cost, enabling affordable mass and scalable ISR operations.

## Desired Attributes

### Primary Desired Attributes
1. **Endurance:** Medium-endurance (3-12+ hours) OR long-endurance (12-24+ hours) per air vehicle
2. **Ruggedized Performance/Weather:** Operation under varied conditions (dust, water, shock, etc.) with appropriate Ingress Protection Rating
3. **Expeditionary Operations:** Ability to refuel, refit, and generate follow-on sorties from austere locations with minimal reliance on fixed infrastructure, established airports, or prepared runways
4. **Payload Capabilities:** Ability to carry and power single or multi-int payloads (EO/IR, SAR, SIGINT) providing decision-quality data; specify SWaP constraints and currently integrated payloads
5. **Cost:** Maximize affordable mass and scalable operations through Government-owned, ISR-as-a-Service, or alternative business models

### Secondary Desired Attributes (Not Required)
- 3rd party Command & Control (C2) integration with technical data package (interface documentation and SDK/API)
- Autonomy features to reduce operator workload (e.g., automated detection alerting)
- Modular Open Systems Approach (MOSA) for integration with Government architectures and Project AVO (Autonomous Vehicle Orchestrator)
- Transportability via expeditionary means (truck, military helicopter) or standard ISO/MIL containers/pallets
- Plan, setup, and launch within three hours of offload
- Minimal manpower requirements for launch, recovery, maintenance, logistics, and operations
- Beyond visual line of sight (BVLOS) operation in varied environments (SATCOM, RF-contested environments)
- Reliable navigation in GNSS-degraded and/or denied environments
- Ability to communicate sensor data via Government-directed radios and waveforms

## Evaluation Criteria

Evaluators will assess:

1. **Sensor Performance:** Available modalities (EO, MWIR, LWIR, SAR), data quality, latency, field of regard/view, and performance of awareness, detection, tracking, targeting, and target custody. Identify performance tradeoffs, limitations, and currently integrated vs. requiring additional integration sensors.

2. **Flight Performance:** Endurance relative to gross take-off weight and available payload, altitude, speed, and signature (e.g., acoustic)

3. **Expeditionary Performance:** Complete infrastructure needed, total operational/logistics footprint, personnel and equipment required for maintenance and operations

4. **Cost:** Mission effect achievable per dollar of total lifecycle cost. **Government may favor lower-cost solutions providing sufficient capability at greater scale over higher-performing solutions limited by cost.** Total lifecycle cost includes platform, sensors, personnel, operations, and sustainment.

5. **Payload Modularity:** Support for third-party payloads and external C2, fires, and intelligence systems. Identify payload interfaces, SWaP limits, integration constraints.

6. **Architecture Design:** Open, modular architecture enabling Government or third-party integration of hardware, software, payloads, communications, mission systems. Distinguish Government-accessible vs. proprietary interfaces.

7. **Autonomy:** Features reducing operator workload and enabling automated mission execution. Describe demonstrated vs. developmental autonomous behaviors, platform autonomy, collaborative autonomy potential, and integrations with Government/third-party C2UI. Employ MOSA approach.

8. **Command, Control & Communication in Contested Environments:** Reliable C2 and ISR data dissemination for LOS and/or BLOS operations. Describe systems approach for GNSS, communications, operations in DDIL (Denied, Degraded, Intermittent, Low-bandwidth) environments. Describe integrations with Government and third-party systems for C2, battle management, information dissemination. Provide evidence of operation under degraded conditions if available.

9. **Reliability, Maintainability & Availability:** Provide available reliability, maintenance, availability, and flight-hour evidence

10. **Environmental Suitability:** Alignment to relevant MIL or commercial standards (e.g., IP Rating)

11. **Manufacturing Readiness:** Ability, approach, and constraints/risks to producing systems quickly and at scale beyond prototype

12. **Future Business Models:** Interest in "ISR as a Service," Contractor-Owned/Contractor-Operated (COCO), Government-Owned/Contractor-Operated (GOCO), and other service-delivery models, including in contested environments

## Challenge Structure & Timeline

### Phase 1: Submissions
- **September 29, 2026:** Solicitation release
- **October 14, 2026:** Submission window closes
- **November 2026:** Selected pitch participants notified

### Phase 2: Pitch Event
- **November or December 2026:** Selected participants present solutions to Government
- **No Later Than December 17, 2026:** Participants selected for fly-offs notified

### Phase 3: Fly-Offs (Capability Demonstrations)

**Fly-Off 1 – Early Q2 FY2027 (Accelerated Demonstration)**
- For companies with operational capability ready by Q2 FY27
- Successful participants receive initial **$250,000 cash prize**
- Eligible for additional cash prizes and/or agreements

**Fly-Off 2 – Q3 FY2027 (Extended Preparation)**
- For companies requiring additional development/integration/preparation time
- Government may invite successful Fly-Off 1 participants to participate
- Successful participants receive **$50,000 cash prize**
- Eligible for additional cash prizes and/or agreements

**Fly-Off Format:**
- Vendors provide aircraft, payloads, and operational teams
- Mission scenarios: various ISR tasks supporting critical information requirements and targeting of static/mobile surface targets across maritime, littoral, and land environments
- Evaluation includes: baseline assessment of maturity/safety/tactical performance, sensor integration and performance, operations in mission-relevant environments (medium and long-endurance and "contested" threat environments), ease of use by uniformed personnel, multi-int fusion, and full mission profile evaluation
- Government reserves right to limit number of aircraft and personnel companies bring
- Flight tests conducted in controlled airspace

## Submission Requirements (Due October 14, 2026)

**Format & Content:**
- Horizontal 16:9 widescreen PDF, maximum 15 slides
- Clearly distinguish:
  - Capability demonstrated today
  - Capability currently undergoing development or integration
  - Projected future capability
- Objective evidence supporting key technical and performance claims (where available)
- Rough Order of Magnitude (ROM) cost estimate

**Required Information:**
- Cost structure and business model
- Indicative pricing for individual systems
- Expected unit-cost reductions at increased production quantities
- Alternative full-service offerings (COCO, ISR-as-a-Service, GOCO, or other service-based models)
- Production plan showing different potential rates and quantities depending on Government needs
- Evidence of ability to reach low-rate production quickly

**Additional Guidance:**
- Include title page with DIU solicitation name, company name, submission date, and point of contact (email, phone, address) - does not count against page limit
- Companies may submit multiple separate solution briefs for different concepts
- Be specific in addressing the problem; show existing technology in production; demonstrate real-world deployment; prove market viability (gross sales/revenue, fundraising, commercialization history)
- Submit electronically via DIU website by deadline; DIU rarely grants extensions

**Selected participants may be required to submit additional technical, training, safety, integration, logistics, or demonstration documentation prior to applicable events.**

## Eligibility & Execution

**Eligibility:**
- U.S. or International Participants subject to security screen before acceptance to fly-off
- Ability to demonstrate solution's capabilities at Department-determined test site if required
- Companies without CAGE code must register in System for Award Management (SAM) with active registration to be eligible for possible Prototype agreement
- Small businesses and non-traditional defense vendors encouraged to apply
- Companies may submit independently or as part of team, partnership, or joint venture

**Execution Costs & Risk:**
- Government does not guarantee selection and will not reimburse submission preparation costs
- Selected participants responsible for costs specifically identified in subsequent guidance (travel, transportation, logistics, equipment preparation, deployment costs not otherwise provided by Government)
- Demonstration locations, performance periods, Government-furnished resources, participant responsibilities, and test requirements may vary by mission-set and will be provided in subsequent guidance
- Participants must be capable of demonstrating applicable objectives within Government-established performance periods

## Intellectual Property Considerations

- Applicants retain ownership of existing IP submitted under Challenge

- For any submission, applicants grant DIU limited license to use IP for testing and evaluation specifically related to the Challenge
- DIU will negotiate with individual competitors for any additional usage, integration, or development contemplated

## Platform Requirements

**Aerial Platforms Only:**
- Medium-endurance (3-12+ hours) OR long-endurance (12-24+ hours) per air vehicle
- Non-aerial autonomous systems (surface or subsurface unmanned maritime vehicles) are NOT eligible

**Submission Categories:**
- Companies may submit single platform for either mission category (medium or long-endurance) and either fly-off (Fly-Off 1 or Fly-Off 2)
- OR one platform addressing multiple fly-offs and categories (must be clearly annotated on page 1 of proposal)
- Multiple submissions in each category allowed (separate solutions submitted as separate proposals)
